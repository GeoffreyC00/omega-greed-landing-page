## PAGE 2 — WELCOME / WHY THIS EXISTS

**Headline:** Welcome

Most riders want to film their rides. Most come home disappointed.

Not because the ride was bad — because there was no plan. No mission. No shot list. No system for gear, audio, story moments, or organizing footage after the ride.

This starter kit is the system I wish I had when I started. It is not YouTube coaching or business advice. It is a practical workflow for motorcycle riders who want to:

- Film better POV and riding footage  
- Capture stronger story moments  
- Bring the right gear without overpacking  
- Organize clips so editing is actually possible  

**You do not film rides. You film stories.**  
The motorcycle is the vehicle into the story.

## PAGE 3 — STORY FIRST, CAMERA SECOND

**Headline:** Story First, Camera Second

Before you touch a camera, ask: **What is the story?**

A Sunday canyon run is a different story than a cross-country road trip. A bike review is different from a “things went wrong in the desert” video.

If you do not know the story, you will ride around collecting random clips. Random clips do not edit well. Random clips do not hold attention.

**Three questions before every shoot:**

1. What will the viewer feel or learn by the end?  
2. What changed from the start of the ride to the end?  
3. What is the one line you could use to describe this video?

The camera serves the story — not the other way around.

## PAGE 4 — THE MISSION FRAMEWORK

**Headline:** The Mission Framework

Before every ride, answer these questions:

**What is the point of this ride?**  
Fun ride? Content day? Road trip leg? Bike test?

**What type of shoot is this?**  
- Casual ride (minimal gear, maybe one clip)  
- Run-and-gun video (one video, light setup)  
- High-effort shoot day (multiple angles, drone, thumbnails)

**One video or multiple?**  
Are you making one focused video or capturing B-roll for several?

**What deliverables do you need?**  
- Thumbnails / still photos  
- Short-form clips (Reels, Shorts, TikTok)  
- B-roll library  
- Drone establishing shots  
- POV only  

Write the mission in one sentence before you leave. Example:  
*“Desert day ride — one 8–12 min adventure video plus 3 short-form clips and thumbnail stills.”*

## PAGE 5 — GEAR LOADOUTS (OVERVIEW)

**Headline:** Gear Loadouts

Match your gear to the mission. Three tiers:

| Loadout | Best for |
|---------|----------|
| **Lightweight** | Quick rides, local runs, one simple video |
| **Adventure** | Desert, road trips, destination videos |

Overpacking slows you down. Underpacking means missing the shot. The next pages break down each loadout.

## PAGE 6 — LIGHTWEIGHT SETUP

**Headline:** Lightweight Setup

**Use when:** Quick rides, local rides, one simple video, testing a new mount.

**Gear list:**
- DJI Osmo Action 5 Pro (or similar action camera)  
- Insta360 camera if available  
- Microphone  
- Basic mounts (handlebar or chest)  
- Extra battery / memory card  

**Workflow tip:** One camera angle + mic. Film POV, a few stops, one talking update. Keep it simple. Done in one battery cycle.

---

## PAGE 7 — ADVENTURE SETUP

**Headline:** Adventure Setup

**Use when:** Desert rides, road trips, destination videos, higher-effort shoot days.

**Gear list:**
- Action camera  
- Insta360 X4/X5 or similar 360 camera  
- DJI Pocket 3  
- Microphone  
- Drone  
- ND filters  
- Extra batteries  
- Memory cards  
- Phone (backup + short-form)  

**Workflow tip:** Assign each camera a job. Action = POV. 360 = rider angles. Pocket 3 = cinematic B-roll. Drone = establishing shots only when legal and safe.

## PAGE 8 — FULL PRODUCTION SETUP

**Headline:** Full Production Setup

**Use when:** Major videos, thumbnail-heavy pieces, sponsor-style content, multi-video shoot days.

**Gear list:**
- Sony A7S III (or main mirrorless camera)  
- DJI Pocket 3

**Workflow tip:** Pack the night before. Charge everything. Format cards. Create the project folder on your drive before you leave.

## PAGE 9 — THE SHOT LIST SYSTEM

**Headline:** The Shot List System

Use the included **Motorcycle Shot List Checklist** on every content ride. Summary:

**Pre-Ride:** Gear on, helmet, gloves, boots, bike startup, cold start, garage setup, loading gear, camera/mount closeups.

**During Ride:** POV, 360 on rider, road signs, lane changes, gas stops, scenic roads, interesting areas.

**Destination:** Establishing shots, bike parked, walkaround, drone (if possible), beauty shots, talking update, environment details.

**Story Moments:** Problems, getting lost, dropping the bike, weather, wrong turns, delays, recovery, reflection, what changed from start to finish.

If it is not on the list and it is not a story moment — you probably do not need it.

## PAGE 10 — COMMON MISTAKES I MADE

**Headline:** Common Mistakes I Made

☐ Forgot the mic — great visuals, unusable audio  
☐ Did not check recording — entire segment lost  
☐ Wrong mounts for the bike or helmet  
☐ Only filmed when things were perfect — missed the real story  
☐ Did not film the recovery after a problem  
☐ No thumbnail photos until I got home (bad light, tired face)  
☐ Rode with no story — just “vibes” footage  
☐ Did not package the video idea before filming  
☐ Stopped shooting B-roll too early  
☐ Dumped everything into one folder — editing nightmare  

Learn from these once. Then use the checklists.

## PAGE 12 — FILE ORGANIZATION WORKFLOW

**Headline:** File Organization Workflow

**Use external hard drives.** Do not edit off a single full SD card.

**Structure:**  
`Month` → `Date - Video Project Name`

**Inside each project folder:**
- Action Camera  
- Insta360  
- Pocket 3  
- Drone  
- Sony / Main Camera  
- Phone  
- B-Roll  
- Notes  
- Exports  
- Done  

**Google Drive for editors:** Copy project folders to Drive. Label by editing priority.  
Example:  
1. I Took My Bike To The Desert  
2. Vegas Ride Home  
3. Road Trip Packing Setup  

See **Folder Structure Template** (Bonus PDF) for copy-paste folders.

## PAGE 13 — EDITOR NOTES WORKFLOW

**Headline:** Editor Notes Workflow

When filming, say **“Editor’s note”** out loud if needed:

- *“Editor’s note: add map here.”*  
- *“Editor’s note: use this as intro.”*  
- *“Editor’s note: cut this section tighter.”*  

Also keep a **Notes** doc inside the project folder:

- Video title idea  
- Hook / opening line  
- Key story beats  
- Music mood  
- Sections to cut  
- Thumbnail idea  

Future you (or your editor) will thank present you.
